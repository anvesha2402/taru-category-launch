# 2. Consent script (read aloud)

> "Thank you for your time. I'm an MBA student at WeSchool doing an independent study on how men choose ethnic wear. It's for a portfolio project about a fictional brand and isn't connected to any company. It takes about 30 minutes and there are no right answers. May I record audio only, for accurate notes? The recording stays with me and is deleted after transcription, and nothing will be linked to your name. You can skip any question or stop at any time. Is that okay?"

If they decline recording: take written notes only and say so in the notes template.

# 3. Laddering: how to probe

Laddering moves from a **product attribute** to its **consequence** for the person, then to the **value** behind it. Keep asking until the answer is about the person, not the product. Usually 2–4 rounds.

| Round | Prompt |
|---|---|
| Attribute → consequence | "Why does that matter to you?" · "What happens when it isn't like that?" |
| Consequence → value | "And why is that important to you?" · "What does that let you do / feel?" |
| Stuck or circular | "Think of the last time it went wrong. What was that like?" |
| Stop when | The answer names a value (being present for family, dignity, respect, confidence, not being judged) |

Worked example from the brief: *soft fabric → no itching during a 6-hour function → I can be present for my family.*

**Do not** suggest answers, name "organic" before question 5 (self-buyer) or question 4 (gifter), or react to answers positively or negatively.

# 4. Self-buyer guide (men aged 40–60, about 30 minutes)

1. Tell me about the last time you bought ethnic wear for yourself: the occasion, where, who else was involved, what you paid, and what nearly stopped you.
   - *Probe: Who had the final say? Did you try it on? Was it bought for one occasion or several?*
2. What do you look for in the fabric? Any bad experiences, such as itching, heat or fit? Ladder: "Why does that matter?" and then "Why is that important to you?"
   - *Probe: How long do you usually wear it at a function? What happens by the end?*
3. How easy is it to find ethnic wear that fits you well? What do you do about fit?
   - *Probe: Do you use a tailor or alterations? Which sizes usually fail, and where (shoulders, belly, length)?*
4. Do you buy anything organic now, such as food or skincare? Why? How do you know it's genuine?
   - *Probe: Have you ever doubted an organic claim? What made you doubt it?*
5. What would "organic" mean to you in clothing? Would it matter? Why or why not?
   - *Ladder the answer. Note whether the reason is personal (skin, comfort, health) or planet-focused.*
6. (Show the three proof cards.) Which would make you believe the claim? What would make you doubt it?
   - *Probe each card: "What would you do with this, if anything?" For the QR card: "Would you actually scan it? When?"*
   - *Ask for a ranking: most to least convincing.*
7. What would you expect to pay for an everyday kurta set? For a wedding-function set? At what point does it feel too expensive?
   - *Probe: Compared with what you usually pay, how much more would "certified organic" be worth, if anything?*
8. Where would you expect to find a brand like this? Would you buy ethnic wear online? What worries you about it?
   - *Probe: Fit, returns, fabric feel, paying in advance?*
9. Is there anything I should have asked?

**Close:** "Could you share the survey with 2–3 people who buy ethnic wear for themselves or for a father? I'll send you the link."

# 5. Gifter guide (ages 25–40, about 25 minutes)

1. Tell me about the last time you bought ethnic wear for your father or father-in-law: the occasion, why that piece, where, and the budget.
   - *Probe: Did he know? Did anyone else help choose?*
2. How do you decide what he'll actually wear?
   - *Probe: Size, colour, fabric? Do you check with anyone?*
3. Has a clothing gift ever gone wrong? What happened?
   - *Ladder: "What did that feel like?" · "Why did it matter?"*
4. Would "certified organic" matter to you as the giver? To him? Why?
   - *Note separately: value to the giver (what the gift says about them) vs value to the wearer.*
5. (Show the proof cards.) Which would make you trust it as a gift?
   - *Probe: Would the proof be something you'd show him, or something only you'd see?*
6. What would you spend on a gift like this? What packaging or presentation would you expect?
7. Where would you buy it: online, in a store, or somewhere else?

**Close:** same referral ask as the self-buyer guide.

# 6. Practitioner guide (about 20 minutes)

**Say before starting:** "Please answer only about general industry practice. I'm not asking about your current or past employer's confidential numbers, and I won't record any."

1. How do new apparel brands get listed in formats like yours?
2. What terms are typical in the industry: outright purchase vs sale-or-return, margin ranges, payment days?
   - *Ask for ranges, not exact figures. Record as "practitioner-reported range" in the register (confidence M).*
3. What are the norms for returns and markdowns?
4. When does ethnic-wear stock need to land for the festive and wedding season?
5. What makes a new brand succeed or fail on the floor?
6. Would a certified-organic claim help sell-through? What proof would store staff need to sell it?

# 9. Outreach messages

**Interview invite (WhatsApp / LinkedIn DM). Self-buyer version**

> Hello [name], I'm Anvesha, an MBA student at WeSchool. I'm doing an independent study on how men choose ethnic wear (kurtas, sets, Nehru jackets). It's for a portfolio project about a fictional brand and isn't linked to any company. Could I speak with you for about 30 minutes, in person or on a call, sometime this week? No preparation needed and nothing is linked to your name.

**Interview invite. Gifter version**

> Hi [name], I'm doing an independent MBA study on how people choose ethnic wear as a gift for their father or father-in-law. If you've bought one in the last 2 years, could I speak with you for about 25 minutes this week? It's for a portfolio project about a fictional brand, and it's anonymous.

**Ask to faculty, classmates and alumni for self-buyer referrals**

> I'm looking for men aged 40–60 who have bought ethnic wear for themselves in the last 2 years, for a 30-minute anonymous interview for my MBA study. If your father, an uncle or a colleague might be willing, could you ask them and share my number?
