**Scope.** Every public claim TARU makes about fibre, certification, environment, comfort or AI-made content. This brief sets the rules; the claims substantiation matrix (D7) applies them claim by claim. Clause numbers are added from the primary texts when the guardrail knowledge base is built (step AI.0). Until then, this brief cites each instrument, not its clauses.

# 1. The rules that apply

| Instrument | Status | What it requires of TARU | Reg. |
|------------------------|----------------|--------------------------------------------------|--------|
| CCPA Guidelines for Prevention and Regulation of Greenwashing or Misleading Environmental Claims, 2024 | In force since 15 Oct 2024 | Generic terms (e.g. "natural", "green", "sustainable") need qualifiers and quantified backing; claims need verifiable evidence and third-party certification; detail may be disclosed via QR code or link | R042 |
| Consumer Protection Act penalties | In force | Up to ₹10 lakh for a first offence, ₹50 lakh for repeats | R043 |
| ASCI Guidelines for Advertisements Making Environmental/Green Claims | In force since 15 Feb 2024 | Ads must say what the certifier assessed, and whether the claim covers the product, the packaging, or only part of either | R044 |
| GOTS label grades | Current | "Organic" needs ≥ 95% certified organic fibre; "made with X% organic" needs ≥ 70% | R047 |
| Legal Metrology garment labelling | In force since 1 Jan 2023 | Maker, country of origin, consumer-care contact, size and MRP on the product and on every e-commerce listing | R045 |
| Draft Textiles Committee labelling regulations | Draft (Mar 2026), not in force | Fibre %, care symbols, QR or barcode; "organic" must meet the applicable standard. Design the tag to comply now | R046 |
| ASCI Guidelines for Responsible Labelling of Synthetically Generated Content in Advertising | **Final**, released 29 Sep 2026; effective 3 months after publication | Banned even with a label: fabricated endorsements or testimonials, deepfakes or likeness without consent, misleading claims about non-existent products, unauthorised copyrighted work. Label needed for synthetic influencers, AI-replicated real people, fabricated events or product demonstrations, AI audio about core features | R050, R085 |
| IT Amendment Rules 2026 (MeitY) | In force since 20 Feb 2026 | AI-generated images posted on platforms need a prominent label | R049 |

# 2. Fibre rules: which fabrics can carry which claim

| Fibre | Can it be "organic"? | Claim TARU may use |
|------------------------------|--------------|--------------------------------------------------|
| Cotton, linen, hemp grown and certified organic | Yes | "Organic [fibre]" if the garment meets the GOTS ≥ 95% grade; otherwise "Made with X% organic [fibre]" if ≥ 70% |
| Bamboo viscose, lyocell (processed fibres) | No | Name the fibre only; in a blend they sit inside a "made with X% organic" claim, never as "organic" themselves |
| Blend with < 70% certified organic fibre | No organic label | Fibre composition only |

# 3. May say / may not say

| TARU **may** say (with a D7 row and evidence) | TARU **may not** say |
|--------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------|
| "Organic cotton, GOTS certified. Licence no. [X], certified by [body]", on a garment meeting the 95% grade | "Organic" with no licence number or certifier |
| "Made with 72% organic cotton" when the certificate supports 72% | "100% organic" unless the garment is literally 100% certified fibre |
| Plain fibre composition: "70% cotton, 30% linen" | "Organic bamboo", "organic lyocell" |
| "Scan to see where this fabric came from", linking to real transaction certificates | "Eco-friendly", "green", "sustainable", "natural", "planet-friendly" with no qualifier and evidence |
| A comfort claim backed by a documented wear trial, with the test described | "Chemical-free", "zero impact", "hypoallergenic", "good for sensitive skin" without test evidence |
| "Free alterations within 30 days", if true | "India's first / only certified-organic menswear brand": Isha Life already sells an organic cotton men's kurta (R032) |
| Packaging claims that name their scope: "Box made from recycled paper" | Claims that blur scope, e.g. "Sustainable packaging and product" |
| AI-generated lookbook images labelled "AI-generated image" | AI-generated testimonials, reviews or customer faces presented as real |

# 4. Implications for the project

1. **Card C's comfort claim needs evidence before any public use.** "Stays soft through a 6-hour function" is a performance claim. It is fine in the survey as a labelled test stimulus. If Card C wins the claim test, the public version needs a documented wear trial or a softer, provable wording, logged in D7.
2. **The starting tagline needs a D7 row.** "Rooted in nature. Refined by time." uses "nature", a generic environmental term. Either qualify it or keep it off product and ad copy.
3. **The swing tag carries the proof.** Legal Metrology fields + GOTS label grade, certifier and licence number + fibre % + QR. Designing to the draft Textiles Committee rules now avoids a reprint.
4. **The supply chain must be certified to the last step.** A GOTS "organic" claim holds only if every processing stage up to the finished garment is certified. The claim stays in the risk column of D7 until the transaction-certificate trail is shown.
5. **AI content rules apply to AI5 and AI6.** Every AI image is labelled; no synthetic testimonials, "customer" faces or endorsements, labelled or not; no likeness of any real person.
